# Fiction comparison protocol 004: frozen canon corpus and rubric

**Frozen:** 2026-09-18 20:38 UTC  
**Evidence category:** fiction analogy only  
**Empirical status:** no experiment, observation, model evaluation, or safety estimate

## Purpose

Preserve the motivating Terminator, Battlestar Galactica, and Star Trek comparison as a disciplined appendix without using fiction as evidence about actual AI systems. This protocol fixes the versions, primary units, coding rubric, exclusions, and stopping rules before any analogy is written.

## Source hierarchy and rights

1. The released audiovisual work in the version named below is the primary source.
2. Official distributor/catalog metadata may establish title, release, and episode identity but cannot replace viewing the primary unit.
3. Production scripts may be used only when an officially released or archive-authenticated version is available and is explicitly labeled as a production document rather than the final cut.
4. Fan wikis, reviews, recaps, interviews, and later creator commentary are secondary context. They cannot establish what occurs on screen and must never silently repair an unavailable primary unit.
5. The future appendix will use short paraphrases and scene/timecode locators. It will not reproduce scripts, subtitles, frames, or long dialogue. All third-party audiovisual works retain their rights.

Metadata starting points, not substitutes for the works: [Paramount+](https://www.paramountplus.com/), [StarTrek.com](https://www.startrek.com/), [Peacock](https://www.peacocktv.com/), and the films’ credited distributors/rightsholders for the specified releases. Availability can change; source identity does not depend on a streaming service remaining online.

## Frozen coding unit

A row is one materially distinct scene, not one line of dialogue. Each row must record:

- corpus ID, exact version, scene description, and start/end timecode from the viewed copy;
- evidence mode: directly depicted action, depicted record/playback, character assertion, institutional ruling, or analyst inference;
- system boundary and embodiment;
- creator/operator and formal authority;
- assigned objective or directive and any observed update mechanism;
- initiating trigger, available resources, and access boundaries;
- communication/cooperation channel and whether participation is authorized;
- adaptation/learning evidence, explicitly separated from scripted capability or a character's assertion;
- monitoring and audit visibility;
- intervention, override, shutdown, or recovery mechanism and whether it succeeds;
- immediate outcome and an on-screen counterexample or contradiction, if any;
- whether the story raises consciousness/personhood, and whether that status is asserted, disputed, adjudicated, or left unresolved;
- nearest engineering/control concept;
- the analogy mismatch that prevents empirical inference;
- coder confidence: high, medium, or low, with a reason.

## Comparison questions

The appendix may compare only these preregistered questions:

1. Where is the system boundary, and what external resources does it require?
2. Who grants authority, and can authority be revised or revoked?
3. Are goals directly assigned, inferred, learned, contested, or changed through experience?
4. Does apparent autonomy reflect access and delegated control rather than an independent motive?
5. What communication enables coordination, and what isolation or permission boundary is depicted?
6. What is observable to supervisors, and what remains hidden?
7. Does a shutdown path exist, who can invoke it, and can it be blocked?
8. Does intervention prevent an action or merely detect/report it?
9. What evidence does the story present for adaptation, identity continuity, distress, consent, or personhood—and what alternative reading remains?
10. Which feature has a legitimate analogue in the project's deterministic toy controls, and where does the analogy break?

## Prohibited inferences

The appendix must not:

- treat plot events as observations of real AI behavior;
- derive probabilities, frequencies, extinction estimates, capability thresholds, or policy efficacy from fiction;
- label a depicted machine conscious or motivated unless the row clearly marks this as an in-universe claim or unresolved interpretation;
- map a fictional faction, species, or individual directly onto current models;
- equate deterministic toy scripts with learned-model emergence;
- use visual similarity, dramatic intent, or a character's confident statement as engineering evidence;
- cherry-pick only successful shutdowns or only catastrophic failures.

## Contradiction and null handling

For each primary unit, actively record at least one of: a counterexample, an explicit ambiguity, a failed analogy, or “none found after full-unit review.” If the relevant primary unit is unavailable, mark it **not coded**; do not fill the gap from a recap. Conflicting cuts remain separate and the frozen cut controls.

## Stopping rule

Review exactly the ten frozen primary units once for candidate scenes and once for contradiction/locator verification. Stop after all rows have version and timecode locators and the contradiction pass is complete. Do not add works because an emerging interpretation is attractive. Corpus changes require a separately dated protocol amendment made before reviewing the added unit.

## Acceptance gate for the later appendix

The appendix is publishable only if every material lore claim has a primary-unit locator, every comparison includes an analogy-limit field, version exclusions are honored, nulls/contradictions are retained, and a final header states that fiction is analogy rather than empirical evidence.
