## ONE TEST: establish a laboratory propulsion/calibration baseline with an explicit reaction reservoir
Candidate: photon rocket / free-space radiation reaction. Momentum reservoir is emitted photons. For optical power P leaving the vehicle, absorbing emission gives F=P/c; ideal retroreflection/normal reflection force on a mirror gives F=(1+R)P/c, approaching 2P/c for R=1. This is established physics and is used here as a falsifiable calibration/control, NOT a novel drive.

Numerical gate: ideal 1 kW reflection gives 6.6712819 microN; 100 W gives 0.667128 microN; 10 W gives 0.066713 microN. A stand with 1 microN 1-sigma force noise would therefore see ideal 1 kW reflection at SNR 6.67, but not 100 W at 5 sigma. Required ideal reflected optical power for 5 microN is 749.48 W. This makes a ~1 kW optical source a measurable but hazardous laboratory calibration, not an 'affordable casual build' recommendation.

Measurement controls: beam must leave or terminate on an independently supported external dump; if source and dump are both on the balance, internal photon momentum cancels in the closed system. Vacuum suppresses convection; reverse beam direction to reverse force; power-off sham; matched thermal dummy load; magnetic/electrostatic cable checks; optical power independently calibrated; blind pulse sequence; measure force versus power and require linear slope consistent with (1+R)/c within predeclared uncertainty. Thermal drift, outgassing, cable forces and beam-dump coupling are rejection channels, not discoveries.

Falsifiable threshold: before testing any unconventional candidate on the same stand, demonstrate a photon-pressure calibration with >=5 sigma detection and fitted force/power slope agreeing with the independently measured optical momentum prediction within combined 2-sigma uncertainty. Any alleged reactionless signal must persist under direction reversal/control conditions and exceed the calibrated 5-sigma force floor while lacking external momentum/thermal/electromagnetic coupling. This threshold does not prove new physics; it only rejects an inadequately characterized stand.

Dimensions: P/c = (J/s)/(m/s)=N. Momentum conservation explicit: photon momentum flux balances apparatus recoil. Boundary condition is open photon flux through the system boundary; closing the optical path on the same balance eliminates net external thrust. Energy source is ordinary electrical/optical power; ideal thrust-to-optical-power <=2/c for reflection force on an external mirror and 1/c for emitted photon rocket thrust.

Primary metrology evidence: NIST Shaw et al. (2021), Measurement Science & Technology, photon-pressure force reference at ~10 nN using ~1 W reflected laser; NIST Photoforce project documents radiation-pressure force proportional to optical power. NASA electric-propulsion thrust stands demonstrate microN-class measurement, including 1–100 microN with 0.1 microN resolution for LISA-class work. These establish measurement feasibility, not novelty.

Tests: added src/photon_baseline.py and six tests for absorber/reflection formulas, inverse power relation, 1 kW value, 1 microN-noise threshold and invalid inputs. Fresh complete reconstructed suite after addition: 90/90 passed in 0.457 s. No CI or independent replication claimed.

Evidence status: physically viable and independently demonstrable baseline propulsion/reaction mechanism, but known physics with extremely poor thrust/power. It is valuable as a calibration standard and conservation-law control for later unconventional candidates, not as the project's desired breakthrough.
