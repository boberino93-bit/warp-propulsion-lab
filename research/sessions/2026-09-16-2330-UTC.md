## ONE bounded falsifiable advance
Define an independent, low-risk mechanical reference-force gate before any unconventional propulsion test. Use apparent deadweight force

`F = m g (1 - rho_air/rho_weight)`

as the SI-traceable reference. At standard gravity `g=9.80665 m/s^2`, with illustrative `rho_air=1.2 kg/m^3` and `rho_weight=8000 kg/m^3`, the masses corresponding to the intended 0.1–1.0 mN calibration range are 10.1987–101.9869 mg. Air buoyancy changes the force by 0.015%, so it is small but explicitly retained.

First-order independent uncertainty propagation is implemented for mass, local gravity, air density, and weight density. An illustrative 101.987 mg reference with 0.01 mg mass uncertainty, `g` uncertainty `1e-5 m/s^2`, air-density uncertainty 0.02 kg/m^3 and weight-density uncertainty 100 kg/m^3 produces about 1.0000008 mN with 0.0981 microN 1-sigma reference uncertainty (~0.00981%). These values are a numerical protocol example, not measured apparatus performance.

The thrust stand must recover both +F and -F. The predeclared gate requires each polarity to satisfy both `|z| <= 3` using combined stand/reference/transfer uncertainty and <=5% absolute fractional bias, plus the correct measured sign. Any filament, pulley, lever or other mechanism that redirects/transfers the deadweight force is explicitly OUTSIDE the reference realization: its transfer uncertainty must be independently measured and entered, never assumed zero.

## Evidence / rationale
NIST states force is traceable through mass and acceleration, with applied deadweight uncertainty including mass, gravity and air density. NIST small-force work reports SI-traceable mass-artifact forces down to about 5 microN, covering this 0.1–1 mN target range. NIST also documented a ~10 nN systematic correlated with a mass-lift mechanism in an electrostatic-force-balance comparison, directly motivating the explicit transfer-mechanism term. NASA has demonstrated non-contact thrust-stand calibration using magnetic force injection for pulsed electric thrusters; that is a useful later cross-check but is not substituted for the present deadweight reference.

Sources:
- NIST, Calibration of Force Transducers: https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/calibration-force-transducers
- Shaw & Pratt, Development of Traceable Small Force Standards (NIST, 2005): https://www.nist.gov/publications/development-traceable-small-force-standards
- Jones et al., Comparison of NIST SI Force Scale to NPL SI Mass Scale (NIST, 2008): https://www.nist.gov/publications/comparison-nist-si-force-scale-npl-si-mass-scale
- NASA NTRS, Non-Contact Thrust Stand Calibration Method for Repetitively-Pulsed Electric Thrusters: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/20110015846.pdf

## Boundary / controls / falsification
This is calibration, not propulsion. Reference-force generation is external to the candidate propulsion boundary. Required controls: zero-load sham, +/− reversal, multiple force levels spanning 0.1–1 mN, randomized/blinded ordering where practical, drift checks before/after, transfer-mechanism characterization, thermal/electromagnetic/cable/airflow/vibration controls, and repeated runs. A calibration that cannot recover known magnitude and sign blocks unconventional testing.
