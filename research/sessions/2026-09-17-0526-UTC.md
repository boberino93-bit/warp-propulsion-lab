## ONE bounded advance
Stress the v0.3.22 polarity-matched sham estimator when SHAM does not perfectly reproduce the reversal-correlated artifact. Added `src/imperfect_sham.py`: a finite multiscale correlated process (sum of AR components from rho=0.3 to 0.999) as a 1/f-like/long-memory stress surrogate, nonlinear quadratic thermal drift, and an explicit sham mismatch fraction `m`, where ON artifact coupling is 1 and SHAM coupling is `1-m`. This is a synthetic stress model, not a claim that it is an exact physical 1/f process.

The corrected estimator retains residual reversal bias `m*b`. With the frozen illustrative gate 14.4194 µN and b=15 µN, a deterministic warning follows immediately: once mismatch approaches 14.4194/15 = 96.13%, the residual artifact alone reaches the gate. Monte Carlo adds correlated noise/drift, so failure can occur earlier. A private independent calculation used the same model and 5,000 trials: false-positive rate was approximately 0.06% at m=0.75, 0.32% at 0.80, 2.32% at 0.85, 8.9% at 0.90, 13.62% at 0.92, 19.66% at 0.94, 26.18% at 0.96, 33.68% at 0.98 and 42.08% at 1.0. These numbers must be reproduced by repository CI/code before being treated as project results.

## Interpretation / falsification
Matched-sham subtraction is only as valid as physical artifact matching. A practical preregistration cannot merely say `use SHAM+/-`; it must measure the ON-vs-SHAM coupling mismatch for reversal-sensitive channels. Under this illustrative stress model, allowing ~90% mismatch is plainly unacceptable even though sham polarity exists. The protocol should reject a candidate test if independent calibration cannot bound residual command-correlated force comfortably below the statistical gate with uncertainty.
