## ONE bounded advance
Added `src/distribution_shift_gate.py`, which freezes the empirical threshold on one synthetic calibration distribution and evaluates independent holdout null/signal campaigns with separately specified mismatch, long-memory-noise amplitude, reversal bias and thermal span. This directly tests distribution shift instead of recalibrating after observing it.

Independent local reproduction of the repository equations with 4,000 calibration and 4,000 holdout trials, alpha=1%, baseline mismatch=0.90, sigma=5 µN, reversal bias=15 µN and thermal span=10 µN gave a threshold of 15.585 µN. Matched holdout FPR was 1.20% with 100% detection of the synthetic 20 µN signal. Increasing only holdout noise to 1.25x raised FPR to 4.075%; 1.5x raised it to 8.0%; 2x to 15.675%. Moving mismatch from 0.90 to 0.95 raised FPR to 5.425%, and 1.00 to 16.0%. Increasing holdout reversal bias only 1.25x raised FPR to 61.525%; 1.5x produced 99.925%. These values are deterministic-seed synthetic calculations, not hardware measurements; CI must independently execute repository tests before merge.

## Decision / hardware implication
The empirical gate is not portable across an unbounded calibration shift. Reversal-correlated bias is especially dangerous because mismatch leaves a signed residual that directly occupies the thrust estimator. Before candidate testing, a physical campaign therefore needs contemporaneous sham/control measurements demonstrating that noise amplitude, reversal coupling and thermal behavior remain inside a preregistered calibration envelope. If controls leave that envelope, candidate data are invalid for a thrust claim rather than grounds to widen/re-fit the gate afterward.
